## 1. Investment Thesis Summary

Apple is in its strongest growth stretch in years: revenue has grown 16–17% y/y for three
straight quarters, driven by the iPhone (+22–23%) and Mac (+29%), with Services still compounding
at 12–14%. Quality is excellent: ~28% net margin, ~$62B net cash, and EPS beats in 8 straight
quarters. **The price already reflects all of that.** At $343.91 the stock trades at ~39× TTM EPS
and ~37× EV/FCF. A reverse DCF says the market is pricing in roughly **17% annual FCF growth for
five years**, against guidance that slows to 9–11% next quarter with gross margin falling ~2–3
points. It's a great business at a full price: **HOLD**, not a new buy at these levels.

## 3. Company Overview & Moat

- **Business:** iPhone (~50% of revenue), Services (~28%, ~75% GM, so roughly 45% of gross profit),
  Mac, iPad, and Wearables/Home. 1.5B+ paid subscriptions.
- **Moat: Wide.** Ecosystem lock-in (iOS + iCloud + Watch + AirPods), brand pricing power, an
  installed base of 2B+ active devices that monetizes through Services, and supply-chain scale.
- **2026 drivers:** an iPhone upgrade cycle, the low-cost **MacBook Neo** expanding Mac's reach,
  and Services pricing.
- **Leadership:** John Ternus now runs the company as CEO (Tim Cook signed the Jan-2026 release),
  with Kevan Parekh as CFO. Leadership-transition risk is low because Ternus was the hardware chief.

**Porter's Five Forces**

| Force | Intensity | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| New entrants | L | scale, ecosystem, and silicon design are huge barriers |
| Supplier power | M | memory-cost inflation is compressing FQ4 GM; TSMC dependency |
| Buyer power | L | premium pricing holds; upgrade cycle is strong |
| Substitutes | M | AI-native devices/agents are a long-dated threat; Apple's AI capex is light |
| Rivalry | M | Android premium tier, China domestic brands |

for years. Still, the gap is large.

**Valuation verdict: Overvalued** on DCF and on its own history; roughly fairly valued against the
analyst consensus.

- **Earnings quality:** high. CFO/NI is consistently above 1.0×. Watch the one-off tariff refund
  in FQ3.
- **ROIC vs. WACC:** ROIC is far above 100% because invested capital (equity + debt − cash ≈ $45B)
  is tiny against ~$130B of NOPAT. WACC is ~8.5–9% (CAPM: Rf ~4.2%, β ~1.0–1.1, ERP ~4.5–5%;
  these inputs are assumptions, not retrieved). **Massive value creation.**
- **DuPont:** ROE (~120%) is driven by margin (~28% net) and by an equity multiplier inflated by
  buybacks. Leverage risk is low because Apple holds net cash.

## 6. Management Assessment

- **Capital allocation:** best-in-class return of capital (~$110B in FY25 between buybacks and
  dividends, plus a new $100B authorization). Buybacks at ~39× earnings are less accretive than
  those done at 15–25×.
- **Guidance credibility:** strong. EPS beat in 8 of 8 quarters, and guidance tends to be
  conservative.
- **AI strategy:** capex (~$11–13B/yr) is a fraction of its megacap peers'. That is good for FCF
  today, but it is the main strategic open question.
- **Alignment:** large RSU-based pay. Insider selling is routine 10b5-1 activity, which carries
  little signal.
